Does it land?

Five people, one sitting each, nothing explained. Decide what success means before anyone plays, then let the codes say whether it happened.

Pass criteria, fixed before testing

These are the claims the piece makes. Write them down before the first session and don't move them afterward. A criterion that fails is the most useful result you can get.

ClaimPasses when
The thesis is feltAt least 4 of 5 one-sentence summaries say that delay breaks a shared moment or real-time talk, or that being together across it needs a different form.
Ava has an inner lifeMedian “I cared what happened to Ava” is 4 or higher, and at least 4 of 5 testers name a specific detail of her life.
The thesis beats carry itAt least 3 of 5 pick one of the four thesis beats as the moment that hit hardest: the song arriving late, the silence, the letters from the silence, or singing on the beat.
The arc holdsAll 5 finish, no tester waits more than 20 seconds at any single prompt (writing the last letter doesn't count), and at most 1 reports being lost.
They investAt least 3 of 5 write during the silence, which is optional and never prompted beyond the text box.
The mechanism is understoodMedian “I understood why the delay changes how people talk” is 4 or higher.

Running a session

  1. Pick five people who haven't heard you describe the project. At most one CS student; mix ages if you can.
  2. Use a laptop and headphones in a quiet room. Open the piece with #playtest at the end of its address: open it in playtest mode.
  3. Ask if they're happy to try an interactive piece and answer four anonymous questions. Use initials only.
  4. Read this, word for word, then stop talking:

    This is an interactive piece. Once it starts I can't help, and there are no wrong moves. When it ends it will ask four questions.

  5. Sit slightly behind them. Note the time of any laugh, sigh, lean-in, phone check or question. Don't answer questions; say “do whatever feels right.”
  6. After the four questions, ask aloud: “What would you change?” and “Who would you send this to?” Write down their words.
  7. Paste their code into the scorer below.

What this does and doesn't show

It shows whether the piece does what it claims for five real people: whether they can state the idea, remember Ava as a person, and get through it unaided.

It is informal playtesting of a creative work, not a research study. Five people can't support statistical claims, and their answers shouldn't appear as findings in the paper without a review board's approval. Quote them on your portfolio only with their permission.

The code each tester produces holds their choices, timings, ratings and written answers. It never holds what they typed to Ava.
