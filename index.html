Why it falls apart — and what you build instead

At Mars's farthest a word takes over 22 minutes to arrive and 22 more to answer. By the time a reply reaches you, you've said three more things; by the time they're heard, they answer a question you've forgotten asking. Push out to Jupiter or Proxima and even that collapses — there is no "now" two places can share. Turn-taking, the thing a conversation is made of, becomes physically impossible.

That's the point of latency-native media, and it's the second toggle above. When you can't share a moment, you stop trying — and design for it. Messages become self-contained capsules: each carries its own context, expects to arrive out of order, and never asks to be answered now, because now doesn't exist across the gap. The frustration you feel in Live mode is the design brief.
